1 · Language, original and translation

What you are searching

The corpus holds each work in the language it was written in. That is 1,249 volumes from forty-one authors: mostly German, then English and French — with Latin, Czech, Finnish, Danish, Portuguese and others besides. The count per author appears on that author's card, under Languages, and comes from the server at every publication: it is not a number typed by hand.

The search always runs in the original language. It is the author's own wording, with no translator's layer in between — which is also why a term in another language finds nothing.

What you get back

The conversation runs in your language. The clone answers in whichever language you wrote in, and switches when you switch.

The quotation comes twice: the original, and below it a rendering into your language. That rendering is produced on the spot by the clone itself, and is declared as such — it is not a published translation by a third party.

Why the rendering is made on the spot instead of taken from an edition

It is not a technical limitation. It is the law.

Brazilian Law 9,610/98, art. 14

“Whoever adapts, translates, arranges or orchestrates a work that has fallen into the public domain holds authorial rights over that version, and may not oppose another adaptation, arrangement, orchestration or translation, unless it is a copy of their own.” translated here for reading; the binding text is the Portuguese original

An original being in the public domain does not release its translations. The translator holds rights of their own, running for the term of art. 41 (seventy years from the translator's death) or art. 43 (seventy years from publication, where the translation is anonymous). For 2026 the practical test is: protection has expired if the translator died in or before 1955, or, for an anonymous translation, if it was published in or before 1955.

What Compendia does about it, and you can verify each one:

  1. Free translations go in — those that have entered the public domain in their own right, with the translator and the date recorded in the volume map.
  2. Protected translations stay out. In case of doubt, the volume does not go in. A gap beats a falsehood. Today 211 volumes are held back over translation rights not yet confirmed, even though they exist in the corpus.
  3. The clone never reproduces a published translation. When you ask for the passage in your language, it renders it on the spot, from the original held in the corpus.

The number held back, and the reason for each, appears on every author's card. It changes when the corpus is re-indexed, and it is read from the server at every publication.

What that means in practice, said plainly

You are buying access to a corpus in foreign languages, with an interpreter that works on the spot and shows you the original alongside. If what you need is a published English edition, ready to cite in a bibliography, this is not that: for that kind of reference the clone will point you where to look — world catalogues and national libraries — and will not invent a publisher, a translator, a year or an ISBN.

2 · The subscription

The subscription is annual: it gives access to the Bibliotheca for one year, counted from the moment payment is confirmed.

What happens when it expires

The key pauses. It is not revoked. When the renewal is paid, the same key starts working again — you do not reinstall anything or redo any setup.

You get an e-mail warning at the address used for the purchase, seven days before expiry. Once the date passes there are still three days of grace in which everything keeps working — and a second warning. Only after that does the key pause.

Renewal

Renewal is charged automatically by the payment platform, using the same method as the purchase, and our system recognises it on its own: the key carries on without interruption.

New authors

An author who enters the corpus during your subscription is already included, at no extra cost, for anyone subscribed to the full Compendia Bibliotheca.

How to cancel

Write to contato@compendia.com.br. There is no form, no minimum term and no penalty.

Cancelling means not renewing. The key carries on to the end of the period you have already paid for and pauses at expiry. Cancelling by itself does not return money for the period under way — for that, see item 3.
