Privacy Policy

Version 7 · 1 October 2026 · applies to compendia.com.br and to the service at corpus.compendia.com.br

Compendia is a corpus of works by classic authors, in the public domain, which artificial intelligence assistants (Claude, ChatGPT and the like) consult in order to locate and quote passages. The service is operated in Brazil by 51.117.605 THIAGO LUZ, CNPJ (Brazilian company registration number) 51.117.605/0001-06.

There are two different situations here, and they handle data differently. Searching the corpus neither requires nor produces data that identifies you. Buying does: to issue your key, we need to know who bought it. The sections below say which is which.

Who is responsible for your data

The service is operated in Brazil by 51.117.605 THIAGO LUZ, CNPJ 51.117.605/0001-06. It is the party named under Contact, at the end of this page.

Payment is a separate matter: Greenn runs the checkout and is responsible for the payment data, under its own policy. Card details do not pass through us. The other companies that receive data, and what each one receives, are listed in Who else receives data, and what.

What reaches the server

When you talk to an author, the assistant you are using sends the server what the search needs: the term or passage being looked for, the author and, where relevant, the volume and page requested. The rest of your conversation does not arrive, nor does your name, nor your account details on the AI platform.

As with any service on the internet, the server automatically records the originating IP address, the time, the route called and the identifier of the program that called it. Those records exist for operation and security.

The access key

Access works through a key that identifies the licence — which authors are unlocked — and not the person. The key is held by whichever AI platform you configured, and, when you search, travels only between that platform and this server.

For how long

Technical access records (the server logs) are deleted automatically within 14 days. The term you searched for is not stored in any database and is not linked to you; it exists only in those technical records, for the same period.

Hostinger makes automatic backup copies of the server, one a week. Those copies are stored in Brazil, contain the technical records and are replaced automatically within 3 weeks. So a technical record may last up to about 5 weeks in total: up to 14 days on the server and up to 3 weeks in the copy.

That period applies only to the technical records. Sales records — e-mail, order and amount — are not deleted after 14 days: they are kept for 5 years from the sale, as a sales record. This is stated in How long we keep the record.

Measurement of browsing (cookies)

This site has three measurement tags. They are switched on only after you click «Accept» on the cookie notice. Without your acceptance, none of the three is loaded.

  1. Google Ads tag — identifier AW-18444114151.
  2. Meta Pixel (Facebook and Instagram) — identifier 981095944998918.
  3. Google Analytics 4 — identifier G-ZFCGP6744X.

Why the Google Ads tag and the Meta Pixel exist: Compendia has started advertising, and these are what make it possible to know that a visit came from an ad we paid for. Without them there is no way to separate someone who arrived through search from someone who arrived through an ad.

Why Google Analytics 4 exists: to understand where visits come from and which pages are read, before a person moves on to the payment platform — a different question from the one the two tags above answer.

What they do: after you accept, all three set a cookie in your browser and tell Google and Meta that this page was opened. Google Analytics 4 sends Google the browsing data for this page. The data is handled by them, each under its own policy, and it goes to servers outside Brazil.

Legal basis: consent (art. 7, I, of the LGPD). What authorises these cookies is your «Accept», and nothing else. Because the Google Ads tag and the Meta Pixel send this data outside Brazil, the transfer for those two also rests on your consent. The Google Analytics 4 transfer is described in Transfer outside Brazil.

What still does not exist: there is no heat mapping, no session recording and no usage profile built by us. The purchase happens off this site, on the payment platform. The Meta Pixel receives no data about the purchase — not whether it happened, not the amount. Google does, but only after the purchase and only by the route described in Measuring whether a purchase came from an ad — not through these tags.

How to choose, and how to change your choice: the choice is yours, on the cookie notice. To change it later, use the «Cookie preferences» link in the footer of any page. Whoever does not accept loses nothing: nothing on the site stops working. The Bibliotheca, the search and your key are indifferent to these tags. Blocking third-party cookies in your browser, or using any ad blocker, also stops all three from loading.

Please note: this choice does not affect the sending described in Measuring whether a purchase came from an ad. That happens on the server, after the purchase, and does not go through the browser.

What we do not do

Nothing is sold. There is no marketing outreach: whoever buys receives the key and the notices belonging to their own subscription, and nothing beyond that. Of the purchase data, one piece of information leaves here for a purpose other than delivering your purchase, with a single recipient and a single purpose: Google receives the buyer's e-mail address in scrambled form, to measure whether the purchase came from an ad. The details are in Measuring whether a purchase came from an ad. The companies that deliver the service, and the encrypted backup copies, are listed in Who else receives data, and what.

When you buy

Payment is processed by Greenn, which operates the checkout. Card details do not pass through us — they go straight to them, and what Greenn does with them is set out in their own policy.

Once a purchase is approved, the payment platform tells us your name, your e-mail and, where you provide them, your tax number and telephone. We keep that data to issue and maintain your access key, to know what it unlocks and until when. There is a second use, of the e-mail address only, described in the next section. Beyond those two, there are only the backup copies, described in How long we keep the record.

Measuring whether a purchase came from an ad

This measurement starts working after this version is published; no purchase made before it is sent. When Greenn tells us that a purchase has been approved, the server adds a row to a Google spreadsheet containing: your e-mail address scrambled by a mathematical formula (SHA-256), the date and time of the purchase, the total amount, the currency and the order number.

Scrambling protects the address, but it does not make the data anonymous. The e-mail is never sent in the clear. But anyone who already has your e-mail address can compute the same code and recognise it — which is precisely how the measurement works. It therefore remains personal data, and is treated as such.

What it is for: Google Ads reads the spreadsheet, at a scheduled time, and compares the code with that of anyone who clicked one of our ads while signed in to a Google account. If they match, the purchase is attributed to that click. That is the only purpose. Anyone who was not signed in to a Google account when they clicked is not identified by this method.

The order number is in the spreadsheet only so that the same sale is not sent twice. Google Ads does not use it.

What is not sent: your name, tax number, telephone, the e-mail in the clear, and what was bought. The Meta Pixel receives none of this.

Where it is processed: Google may process this data on servers outside Brazil. The spreadsheet sits in a Google account.

Legal basis: legitimate interest (art. 7, IX, of the LGPD) — knowing whether money spent on advertising brought in the sale. This measurement is a different thing from the cookies above: it uses no cookie, does not go through the browser and does not depend on the «Accept» on the cookie notice. The data sent is the minimum necessary, and you can object, as below.

For how long: the row is deleted from the spreadsheet within 90 days — the period in which Google still connects a click to a purchase. After that it serves no purpose.

How to refuse: write to contato@compendia.com.br asking us not to use your e-mail in this measurement. The row is deleted from the spreadsheet and your e-mail stops being sent for future purchases; your key carries on working. This refusal is different from refusing cookies, above: blocking a cookie does not stop this sending.

The sending program keeps the recipient's number and name; the text of the message is not kept.

These are messages about your purchase: the key, and then the notices belonging to the subscription itself. They do not go onto a marketing list.

Google, Meta, Cloudflare, GitHub and Resend are companies that may process data outside Brazil, and WhatsApp belongs to Meta. What each one receives is set out in Who else receives data, and what. As for Greenn, we do not state where it processes data: what applies is in its own policy.

Transfer outside Brazil

The Brazilian General Data Protection Law (art. 33) says in which cases personal data may go to another country. These are the cases in which the transfer has a declared basis:

  1. Google and Meta, in the browser cookies (Google Ads tag and Meta Pixel): you consent, specifically and prominently, by clicking «Accept» on the cookie notice, knowing that this data goes outside Brazil (art. 33, VIII). Without your acceptance, this data is not sent.
  2. Google Analytics 4: the same basis as the cookies above — it is only switched on after you click «Accept», knowing the data goes outside Brazil (art. 33, VIII).
  3. Cloudflare, Resend and WhatsApp, for those who buy: the transfer is necessary to perform the contract you make when you buy, or steps you ask for before buying (art. 33, IX, combined with art. 7, V).

Other data may also be processed outside Brazil: by Google, in purchase measurement (the spreadsheet with the scrambled e-mail) and in the backup copies kept on Google Drive; and by Cloudflare and GitHub, when delivering the pages to those who only visit. What each one receives is in Who else receives data, and what.

How long we keep the record

For as long as your licence exists, because the record is what makes the key work. You can ask for deletion at any time — and, if you do, the key stops working with it, since the record is what sustains it.

Deletion removes, on our server, your record and the key, and removes your row from the ad-measurement spreadsheet. Two things stay: the sales record, described below, and a trace showing that the request was carried out — your scrambled e-mail, on a list that stops a future purchase from being measured again, and the date it was handled.

That list of scrambled e-mails holds only people who refused the measurement or asked for deletion. It exists so that a purchase of yours is not sent to measurement again. It is kept while there is an active subscription or renewal, and for up to 90 days after it expires. After that, the entry leaves the list. Legal basis: legitimate interest (art. 7, IX).

Sales record: the e-mail, the order number and the amount of each purchase are kept for 5 years from the sale, as a sales record — and not for the 14 days of the technical records. Keeping them is a legal obligation (art. 7, II, of the LGPD), so a deletion request does not shorten it.

Deletion has limits, and they are written here so that you know them. It does not reach the sales record, above. It does not reach what Greenn, Resend and WhatsApp keep: that is asked of each of them. Nor does it reach the full copies of the server, made by hand, occasionally and on no schedule. They are encrypted and kept outside it: the 3 most recent on the responsible party's disk and 3 on Google Drive. The record is in them and there is no period in days: the data leaves them only when the copies are replaced by newer ones.

On what legal basis

The Brazilian General Data Protection Law (Law 13,709/2018) asks for a legal basis for each use of data. These are ours, by purpose. Each use has one basis only:

  1. The record, the key and delivery of the key — by e-mail or WhatsApp — and the subscription notices: performance of a contract (art. 7, V).
  2. Technical server records and backup copies: legitimate interest (art. 7, IX).
  3. Delivering the pages to those who only visit (Cloudflare and GitHub): legitimate interest (art. 7, IX), so that the site works and stays secure.
  4. Browser cookies (Google Ads tag, Meta Pixel and Google Analytics 4): consent (art. 7, I), given when you click «Accept» on the cookie notice. As in Measurement of browsing (cookies).
  5. Purchase measurement, done by the server: legitimate interest (art. 7, IX), as in Measuring whether a purchase came from an ad.
  6. The list of scrambled e-mails of people who refused the measurement or asked for deletion: legitimate interest (art. 7, IX), for the period described in How long we keep the record.
  7. Sales record (e-mail, order and amount), kept for 5 years from the sale: compliance with a legal obligation (art. 7, II).

For the uses based on legitimate interest, you can object through the contact below. For the uses based on consent, you can withdraw it at any time through the «Cookie preferences» link in the footer. For backup copies and the sales record, the limit described in How long we keep the record applies.

Security

These are the measures in place today:

  4. Backup copies of the whole server are kept encrypted, outside the server.

No measure brings the risk down to zero.
